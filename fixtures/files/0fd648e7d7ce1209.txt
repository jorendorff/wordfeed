<h3>
izzard
</h3>
<p>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">PRONUNCIATION:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
(IZ-uhrd)
<a href="https://wordsmith.org/words/izzard.mp3"><img src="https://wordsmith.org/words/images/sound-icon.png" style="border:0;height:24px;width:32px;vertical-align:middle" alt="Listen to pronunciation"></a>
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">MEANING:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
<i>noun</i>: The letter Z.
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">ETYMOLOGY:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
An alteration of earlier ezod/ezed, probably from French et zede (and
zed), said at the end of the alphabet. Earliest documented use: c. 1726.
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">NOTES:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
We went looking for a word with exactly five Zs but couldn&#8217;t find
one. We could stretch zzz into zzzzz, but we wanted to end the week awake.
So here&#8217;s a word for the letter Z itself, izzard: two Zs in its spelling,
and the letter Z in its meaning.<br><br>

Izzard survives chiefly in expressions such as &#8220;from A to izzard&#8221; (from
beginning to end) and &#8220;to not know A from izzard&#8221; (to be ignorant).<br><br>

A similar alphabet recitation gave us ampersand, from &#8220;and per se and&#8221;:
&amp; by itself stood for the word &#8220;and&#8221;.
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">USAGE:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
&#8220;He doesn&#8217;t know A from izzard when it comes to women.&#8221;<br>
Dan Kennedy; Z; Edited by Meredith Broussard; <i>The Encyclopedia of Exes</i>;
Crown; 2005.
</div><br>
