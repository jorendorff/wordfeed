<h3>
priapean
</h3>
<p>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">PRONUNCIATION:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
(pry-uh-PEE-uhn)
<a href="https://wordsmith.org/words/priapean.mp3"><img src="https://wordsmith.org/words/images/sound-icon.png" style="border:0;height:24px;width:32px;vertical-align:middle" alt="Listen to pronunciation"></a>
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">MEANING:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
<i>adjective</i>:<br>
1. Phallic.<br>
2. Lewd; salacious.
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">ETYMOLOGY:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
After Priapus, a god of fertility in Greek and Roman mythology. Earliest
documented use: 1623.
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">NOTES:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
Priapus was usually depicted with an oversized erect penis, and his
statues served as guardians of gardens. They were hard to miss. His name
also gives us priapism, a prolonged, often painful erection that occurs
without sexual stimulation or persists after it has ended.
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">USAGE:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
&#8220;[<i>Baby Doll</i>] describes, with a degree of Priapean detail that might
well have embarrassed Boccaccio, how the syndicate&#8217;s manager ...
gets his revenge.&#8221;<br>
New Picture; <i>Time</i> (New York); Dec 24, 1956.
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">A THOUGHT FOR TODAY:</div>
The only maxim of a free government ought to be to trust no man living with
power to endanger the public liberty. -John Adams, 2nd US president (30 Oct
1735-1826)
