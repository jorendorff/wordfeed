<h3>
irrupt
</h3>
<p>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">PRONUNCIATION:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
(i-RUHPT)
<a href="https://wordsmith.org/words/irrupt.mp3"><img src="https://wordsmith.org/words/images/sound-icon.png" style="border:0;height:24px;width:32px;vertical-align:middle" alt="Listen to pronunciation"></a>
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">MEANING:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
<i>verb intr.</i>:<br>
1. To burst or break in.<br>
2. (Of a population) To increase suddenly in number.
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">ETYMOLOGY:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
 From Latin irrumpere, from in- (into) + rumpere (to break). Earliest
documented use: 1855.
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">NOTES:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
Originally, to irrupt was to burst in; to erupt was to burst out.
Since then, irrupt has broken into erupt&#8217;s territory.
</div><br>

<div style="font-family:Verdana; color:#555555; font-size:13px;">USAGE:</div>
<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
&#8220;The odd stories of Yoko Ogawa, a Japanese author, irrupt into the
ordinary world as if from the unconscious or the grave.&#8221;<br>
Slightly Off; <i>The Economist</i> (London, UK); Jan 26, 2013.
<br><br>

See more usage examples of <a href="https://www.vocabulary.com/dictionary/irrupt">irrupt</a> in Vocabulary.com&#8217;s <a href="https://www.vocabulary.com/dictionary">dictionary</a>.
</div><br>
